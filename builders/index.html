How a New Landscape Goes In

1. Final Grade & Layout

We start where the concrete and fence crews finished. The pad gets shaped to positive fall away from the slab, the beds and turf lines get marked out, and utilities get located before a shovel goes in the ground. Everything after this depends on getting it right here.

  • ▸ Positive fall away from the foundation
  • ▸ Bed and turf lines marked
  • ▸ Utilities located and flagged
  • ▸ Construction spoil hauled off

2. Drainage

The step that generates callbacks when it gets skipped. French and channel drains go in where the lot needs them, tied to daylight, while the ground is still open — because the cheapest time to fix water is before sod hides where it goes.

  • ▸ French & channel drains
  • ▸ Swales and regrading
  • ▸ Downspouts tied in
  • ▸ Done before sod, not after a complaint

3. Irrigation

Zones laid out to how the lot actually gets sun, valves and wiring set, heads placed for head-to-head coverage. Texas requires a licensed irrigator to install a system for compensation and ours is current. This goes in while the ground is open — it is the cheapest it will ever be.

  • ▸ Licensed irrigator on the work
  • ▸ Zoned to sun and slope
  • ▸ Smart controller & rain sensor
  • ▸ Coverage tested before we leave

4. Flowerbeds & Trees

Beds shaped and cut to a clean edge, shrubs set to spacing, trees sized to the elevation and planted at the right depth rather than dropped in a hole. If the development sets tree counts or minimum caliper, we plant to the packet.

  • ▸ Beds shaped, cut and edged
  • ▸ Planted to depth and spacing
  • ▸ Trees sized to the elevation
  • ▸ Built to HOA tree counts

5. Sod Installation

St. Augustine, Bermuda, or Zoysia over prepared soil — not laid straight onto compacted construction clay, which is why so much new-build turf is dead by August. Seams tight, rolled, and watered in before we hand it over.

  • ▸ Soil prep, not sod over clay
  • ▸ Grass matched to sun and traffic
  • ▸ Seams tight and rolled
  • ▸ Watered in and scheduled

6. Final Walkthrough

We walk it with you before the buyer sees it. Punch list handled, edges cut, mulch topped, the street and the neighbouring lots swept, and every pallet and off-cut hauled away. You should not be finding our mess on a finished street.

  • ▸ Punch list before closing
  • ▸ Edges cut, mulch topped
  • ▸ Street and adjacent lots cleaned
  • ▸ Watering schedule handed to the buyer

Common Questions

Are you licensed and insured, and can we get a COI?

Yes to both. Godoy's Wolves is licensed and insured, and our irrigation licensing is current — Texas requires a licensed irrigator to install or repair a system for compensation. Ask and we will send a certificate of insurance naming you as additional insured before we set foot on the lot.

Do you take one custom home, or only volume work?

Either, and they are scheduled differently. A custom home gets a crew that stays on it rather than being pulled to another address halfway through — on a house like that the finish is the whole point. Volume work gets resourced to the release schedule you give us. Send either one and we will tell you honestly what we can hold, because a sub who overpromises capacity is the one who misses your date.

Can you work from a landscape architect's plan?

Yes. On a custom home the plan usually already exists and the job is installing it faithfully — right species, right sizes, right spacing, set where the drawing puts them. If something on the plan will not survive the soil or the exposure on that lot, we say so before it goes in the ground rather than after it dies.

What about gated or private neighborhoods?

Common on the work we do. Give us the access process and we follow it. Crews keep the street and the neighboring lots clean, stage material where you tell us to, and haul off at the end of the day rather than leaving pallets and spoil sitting on a finished street.

Will you build to the architectural review committee's requirements?

Yes. Private neighborhoods usually set tree counts, minimum caliper, screening and bed standards, and the committee has to sign off before a closing. Send us the packet and we design and install to it, because failing that review delays your closing and that lands on you.

When do you need to be on the lot?

After final grade is set and the exterior trades are clear, before the walkthrough. Landscape installed while concrete and fence crews are still working gets driven over and paid for twice. Give us the closing date and we schedule backward from it.

Why does grading come before irrigation?

Because everything downstream sits on it. Trenching a system into a pad that still has to be reshaped means digging the same ground twice, and drainage pipe has to be under finished grade to work at all. We shape the lot and mark the lines first, then the system goes in once and stays where it was put.

What happens if something dies or a drain does not hold?

We come back. Plants and sod are living material and North Texas clay is unforgiving, so some replacement is normal rather than a dispute. Tell us what failed and we will get out there. Callbacks cost you a homeowner conversation you should not have to have.

Do you have to do the whole exterior, or can we use you for part of it?

Either. Plenty of builders start with sod and irrigation and keep their own hardscape sub. We would rather do one part of the scope well than take work we are stretched on.

Can you meet HOA and development landscape standards?

Yes. Most North Texas developments set minimum tree counts, sod coverage, and bed standards before final approval. Send the packet and we design to it, because failing that inspection delays a closing and that lands on you, not the sub.

Who is actually on our jobs?

Rafael Godoy, the owner, walks the lot and prices the work, and you have his number. You will not be handed to an account manager who has never seen the site.

How do we get a number on a plan?

Send the plot plan and the landscape requirements, or just get us the address. We walk it, price the whole exterior as one job instead of a stack of separate trades, and come back with a written number.
