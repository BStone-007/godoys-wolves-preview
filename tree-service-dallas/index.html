Common Questions

How much does tree removal cost in Dallas, TX?

Small trees typically run $150–$500, medium trees $500–$1,500, and large trees $1,500–$4,000+ depending on access and condition. Stump grinding adds $100–$400. Every job gets a free on-site estimate.

Are you licensed and insured?

Yes. Full liability insurance and workers compensation, with certificates available before any work begins.

How fast can you get to Dallas?

Estimates usually go out within the hour during business hours, and hazards get a two-hour response. Storm work runs seven days, and anything on a roof or touching a line is triaged ahead of routine jobs.

Do you grind the stump as well?

Yes, to roughly 12 inches below grade so the ground will take sod or planting afterward. Doing it during the removal costs less than bringing the grinder back on a separate trip.

What happens to the wood and debris?

It leaves with us. Limbs, chips, and the trunk are hauled off and the ground is raked and blown before we go. If you would rather keep the wood cut to length and stacked, say so and we will do that instead.

Do you work with insurance claims after storm damage?

Yes. We photograph the damage before anything is cut and give you an itemised invoice your adjuster can work from. Emergency make-safe work usually goes ahead first and the paperwork follows.

Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Dallas?

Dallas protects certain trees under its landmark and canopy ordinances. Removals of protected species or larger diameters can require a permit. We'll tell you during the free estimate whether your tree qualifies and handle the process with you.

What about oak wilt in Dallas?

Oak wilt is active across Dallas County and spreads through root grafts and fresh pruning wounds. We avoid trimming oaks February–June and seal cuts when oak work can't wait.

Can you work on tight Dallas lots?

Yes, and most of the city is exactly that. Around Lakewood, Oak Cliff, and the M Streets there is no crane access and neighbours sit close on both sides, so removals come down in sections on ropes rather than being felled.

Do you handle trees near power lines?

Work near a service drop gets coordinated with the utility rather than worked around. We will also tell you plainly when a limb is the power company's responsibility instead of billing you for something they will do free.
