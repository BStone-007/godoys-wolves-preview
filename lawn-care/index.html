What a Visit Actually Includes

Mowing

Cut at the right height for the grass you have, not one blade setting for every yard on the route. Bermuda goes low, St. Augustine stays tall, and in August both go higher, because scalped turf in a Texas summer does not come back.

  • ▸ Height set by grass type and season
  • ▸ Sharp blades, no torn tips
  • ▸ Direction alternated to stop rutting
  • ▸ Clippings mulched back or bagged on request

Edging and Trimming

A hard spade line along drives, walks, and beds is what separates a mowed lawn from a maintained one. Trimmed around posts, trees, and anything the mower cannot reach.

  • ▸ Spade-cut edges on drives and walks
  • ▸ Bed lines held all season
  • ▸ String trimming around obstacles
  • ▸ Blown clean before we leave

Bed and Shrub Maintenance

Shrubs shaped rather than sheared flat, beds kept clear, and mulch refreshed before it thins out and lets the weeds through.

  • ▸ Shrub shaping and hedge lines
  • ▸ Bed weeding and clearing
  • ▸ Mulch refresh
  • ▸ Seasonal color swaps

Fertilization and Weed Control

A seasonal program timed to the North Texas calendar. Pre-emergent before the soil warms, feeding through the growing season, and spot treatment for the weeds that get through.

  • ▸ Pre-emergent timed to soil temperature
  • ▸ Seasonal feeding schedule
  • ▸ Broadleaf spot treatment
  • ▸ Grub and pest watch

Seasonal Cleanups

Spring and fall resets that get the property back to a baseline: leaves cleared, beds cut back, debris hauled off rather than piled at the curb.

Common Questions

How much does lawn service cost?

It depends on lot size, how much edging and bed work the property needs, and whether you want weekly or biweekly. Rafael walks the property and gives you a flat per-visit price before anything starts. Our DFW lawn care cost guide on the blog explains what moves the number.

Do I have to sign a contract?

No. Weekly and biweekly service run visit to visit, and you can stop whenever you want. Commercial and HOA properties usually prefer a written schedule, and we will do that where it helps the property manager.

Weekly or biweekly?

Weekly from roughly March through October, when North Texas turf grows fast enough that skipping a week means cutting off too much at once. Biweekly works through the cooler months. We will tell you which one your lawn actually needs rather than selling you the bigger number.

Do you bag the clippings?

We mulch them back by default, because clippings return nitrogen to the lawn and that is free fertilizer. If you would rather they came off the property, say so and we will bag them.

What if it rains on my service day?

We move you to the next dry day rather than cutting a saturated lawn, which tears the turf and ruts the ground. You get a message when the day shifts.

Do you handle sprinklers?

Yes. Our irrigation licensing is current, which Texas requires for anyone repairing a system for pay. The crew flags what the yard is doing between visits, and Rafael diagnoses the system properly rather than guessing from the curb.
