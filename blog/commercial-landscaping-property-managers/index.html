Commercial Landscaping Maintenance: What Every DFW Property Manager Needs to Know

First Impressions Start at the Curb

For commercial properties, landscaping is a direct reflection of management quality. A well-kept landscape tells tenants you care, tells customers the businesses inside are professional, and tells prospects the property is worth leasing. Overgrown hedges, dead turf, and weeds in the parking lot cracks say the opposite.

Studies consistently show quality commercial landscaping can increase property values by 10 to 15 percent. That spend returns measurably.

What a Commercial Maintenance Contract Should Include

At minimum: weekly mowing and edging during the growing season (March through November in DFW), monthly bed maintenance with weeding and mulch touch-ups, quarterly shrub and hedge trimming, seasonal flower rotations two to four times a year, monthly irrigation inspections, and fall/winter cleanup. A good contractor also covers tree trimming, parking lot sweeping, and storm cleanup.

Ask about response times. If a storm drops a limb across your entrance Monday morning, same-day response should be standard rather than a premium add-on.

Red Flags When Hiring

No proof of insurance is the biggest one. If an uninsured worker is hurt on your property, the liability can land on you. Always verify a current certificate. No commercial references is another: residential lawn care and commercial grounds maintenance are different operations. And vague pricing without a detailed scope of work is a recipe for disputes.

Seasonal Strategy for DFW Properties

Spring is pre-emergent weed control and irrigation startup. Summer is irrigation management and heat-stress monitoring through 100-degree stretches. Fall is leaf removal, aeration, and overseeding. Winter is dormant pruning and freeze protection. The best commercial landscapers don't wait for your call. They flag issues during regular visits before small problems become expensive ones.
