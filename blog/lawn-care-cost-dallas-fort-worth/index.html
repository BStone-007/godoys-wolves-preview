What Lawn Care Costs in Dallas-Fort Worth

Lawn service is priced per visit, not per hour

Almost every lawn company in Dallas-Fort Worth quotes a flat per-visit price. You are not buying an hour of labour, you are buying a finished result: cut, edged, trimmed, blown clean. If the crew gets faster at your property over time, the price does not drop, and if a storm makes the job take twice as long, it does not climb either.

That matters when you compare quotes. Two companies can name very different numbers for the same yard because they are including different work, not because one is overcharging.

What actually moves the number

Lot size is the obvious one, but it is rarely the biggest factor. What drives the price up fastest is edge work: linear feet of drive, walk, and bed line that has to be cut by hand every visit. A large open lawn with two straight edges can cost less to maintain than a small yard carved into six curved beds.

Access matters more than people expect. A gate too narrow for a zero-turn means the whole property gets cut with a push mower, which can double the time on site. Steep slopes, retaining walls, and trampolines that have to be moved all do the same thing.

Then obstacles: trees to trim around, playsets, dog runs, raised beds. Each one is a stop, a switch to the string trimmer, and a restart.

Frequency changes the per-visit price too, and not in the direction people assume. Biweekly visits often cost more each time than weekly ones, because two weeks of North Texas growth in May is a heavier cut with more clippings to deal with.

Weekly or biweekly in a Texas summer

From roughly March through October, DFW turf grows fast enough that weekly is the honest recommendation. The reason is the one-third rule: cutting off more than a third of the blade at once stresses the grass, and after two weeks of May growth you cannot get back to your target height without breaking it.

Scalped St. Augustine in July does not recover quickly. It browns, thins, and lets weeds into the gaps, and then you are paying for weed control on top of mowing.

Biweekly is genuinely fine through the cooler months, and a good company will tell you when to switch rather than quietly billing you weekly through January.

What should be included, and what is usually extra

A standard visit should include mowing, edging along hard surfaces, string trimming, and blowing the clippings off drives and walks. If a quote does not say edging, ask, because a lawn that is mowed but never edged looks unkempt within a month.

Usually separate: fertilization and weed control programs, aeration, seasonal cleanups, mulch, shrub shaping beyond a light trim, and hauling away anything bulky. None of that is a hidden fee if it is stated up front, and all of it is a nasty surprise if it is not.

Ask specifically what happens to the clippings. Mulching them back is better for the lawn and free; bagging and hauling them is a real cost.

Why the cheapest quote usually is not

The single biggest thing separating a low quote from a fair one is insurance. If an uninsured worker is hurt on your property, the exposure lands on your homeowner policy. Ask for a certificate of insurance and a license, and treat reluctance as an answer.

The other pattern is the quote that ignores the beds. It looks cheaper for two months, and then the bed lines have crept, the shrubs are shapeless, and you are paying someone else for a reset that costs more than a season of the difference.

Watch for crews that change every week, too. Nobody learns your property, the gate gets left open, and the same sprinkler head gets clipped a third time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is residential lawn care priced in Dallas Fort Worth?

Almost every professional lawn care company in Dallas Fort Worth quotes a flat per visit rate based on lot square footage, linear feet of edging and bed lines, backyard gate access width, and weekly versus biweekly frequency.

Is weekly or biweekly mowing better during a North Texas summer?

From March through October, weekly mowing is recommended across DFW so you never cut more than one third of the grass blade at once, which prevents heat stress and scalping on St. Augustine, Bermuda, and Zoysia turf.
