When to Call an Emergency Tree Service in Dallas-Fort Worth

Recognizing a Tree Emergency

A tree on your house, car, fence, or any structure is an obvious emergency, as is anything blocking your driveway or a road. A tree leaning at a NEW angle after a storm should be treated as urgent even before it falls.

Power lines are the most dangerous scenario. If a tree or branch has fallen on or near power lines, do not approach it, do not try to move it, and keep people back. Call 911 and your utility company immediately. Downed lines can energize the ground around them. Less obvious emergencies: cracked hanging branches (widow-makers), split trunks, and root heave where the root ball has lifted out of the ground.

What to Do Before the Crew Arrives

Keep people and pets back, because internal damage isn't visible and further collapse happens without warning. Document everything for insurance: photos and video from multiple angles, including the break point. If water is entering your home, tarp the opening; insurers expect reasonable mitigation and typically reimburse it.

Call your insurance company promptly. Most homeowner policies cover tree removal when the tree damages a covered structure. A tree that falls in the yard without hitting anything may not be covered.

How Emergency Removal Works

Priorities run safety, then property protection, then cleanup. Cranes may be needed for large trees in tight spaces. The work is methodical, because rushing an emergency removal is how accidents happen. If a tree is on your house with rain coming, a good company gets a crew there within hours. During region-wide storm events, waits stretch as every crew in DFW works around the clock.

Choosing an Emergency Tree Service in DFW

After major storms, out-of-state 'storm chasers' flood the area, many of them uninsured and unaccountable. Stick with a local company: ask for the certificate of liability insurance and workers compensation before work begins, check Google reviews, and get a written estimate even in an emergency.
