How Much Does Tree Removal Cost in Dallas, TX? (2026 Pricing Guide)

What Does Tree Removal Actually Cost in Dallas?

Small trees (under 30 feet) typically cost between $150 and $500 to remove. These include ornamental trees, young oaks, and smaller cedar elms common across North Texas neighborhoods. Medium trees (30 to 60 feet) run between $500 and $1,500. Most mature shade trees in Dallas yards, including live oaks, pecans, and red oaks. Large trees (60 feet and above) cost $1,500 to $4,000 or more: big post oaks, cottonwoods, and mature pecans that require heavy equipment, careful rigging, and experienced crews.

Stump grinding is usually a separate charge, typically adding $100 to $400 depending on the stump diameter. Many companies in the DFW area offer package deals that include stump removal with the tree takedown, which costs less than a return trip.

Factors That Affect Your Price

Size is the biggest factor, but not the only one. A tree in an open backyard with easy equipment access costs less than one wedged between your house and your neighbor's fence. Trees near power lines require utility coordination and specialized techniques. Hardwoods like oaks and pecans are denser than cedar or pine, which means more labor and equipment time.

A dead or dying tree often costs more rather than less. Dead trees are unpredictable, branches snap without warning, and the trunk may be hollow. Emergency removal after a storm always carries a premium for immediate response, though a reputable company still prices it fairly.

How to Save Money on Tree Removal in DFW

Get at least three estimates from licensed and insured companies. Be cautious of prices that seem too good to be true. A crew with a pickup and a chainsaw likely carries no insurance, and if something goes wrong, you're liable.

Late fall and winter are the slowest months for Texas tree services, so pricing improves. Bundling multiple trees earns volume discounts, and asking about stump grinding upfront beats calling someone back later.

Why Choose a Local DFW Company

Local crews know the soil, the species, the weather, which neighborhoods require HOA permits, and how to coordinate with local utilities.
