Dallas Has an Oak Problem

Oak wilt is the single most important thing about trimming trees in Dallas, and most homeowners have never heard of it until a tree is already dying. It spreads underground through root grafts between oaks planted near each other, which describes most Dallas streets, and above ground by beetles drawn to fresh pruning cuts. Those beetles are active February through June. A well-meant spring trim is one of the most reliable ways to introduce it to a healthy tree.

So we do not prune oaks in that window. When a hazard genuinely cannot wait, the cut gets sealed immediately. Everything else is scheduled for dormancy, which is better for the tree anyway.

The other Dallas constraint is access. Around Lakewood, the M Streets, Oak Cliff, and Preston Hollow there is no room to drop anything, no crane access, and a neighbor's roof a few feet the other side of the fence. Trimming here is rope work with the drop zone controlled, which is slower than a suburban lot and priced that way up front rather than discovered halfway through.

Crown Thinning

Opening the interior of the canopy so wind passes through instead of pushing against it. On Dallas's mature live oaks and cedar elms this is the work that prevents limb failure in spring storms.

  • ▸ Interior deadwood taken out
  • ▸ Shape and height unchanged
  • ▸ Staged on older trees

Crown Lifting & Roof Clearance

Getting limbs off roofs, gutters, drives, and alleys. In older Dallas neighborhoods this is most of what we do, and the constraint is always that there is nowhere for a limb to land.

  • ▸ Alley and drive clearance

Emergency Trimming & Storm Hangers

After a Dallas storm the dangerous part is usually still in the tree. Broken limbs caught in the canopy come down first, then the torn stubs get cut back clean so the wound can seal.

Oak Pruning, Scheduled Right

Live oaks, post oaks, and red oaks pruned in dormancy rather than in beetle season, with wounds sealed on anything that could not wait. This is the difference between a trim and a diagnosis two years later.

  • ▸ Scheduled outside February to June

Crape Myrtle Pruning

Thinned and shaped rather than cut back to knuckles. The annual stub-cutting seen across Dallas produces weak whippy growth and flowers that flop in the first rain, and it is entirely avoidable.

Correcting competing leaders and narrow forks while the tree is still small enough for it to be cheap. Dallas replanted heavily after the 2019 tornado, and those trees are exactly the right age for this now.

Common Questions

How much does tree trimming cost in Dallas?

It is priced per tree after Rafael sees it. Size and access drive the number more than anything else: the same live oak costs far less on an open lot than it does over a roof in the M Streets with no crane access and a neighbor's fence three feet away. The estimate is free, on site, and you get a firm price before work starts.

When can my Dallas oaks be trimmed?

Outside February through June. Oak wilt is active across Dallas County, and the beetles that carry it between trees are drawn to fresh pruning wounds during those months. Late winter dormancy is the ideal window. If a limb is an actual hazard we will take it whenever you call and seal the cut immediately.

How do I know if my tree has oak wilt?

The classic sign on live oaks is veinal necrosis, where the leaf veins turn yellow or brown while the tissue between them stays green, followed by heavy leaf drop out of season. Red oaks usually die fast, often within weeks, sometimes with a fungal mat splitting the bark. If a mature oak on your street died quickly last season, the oaks either side of it are worth looking at now, because it moves through connected roots.

Can you trim a tree with no truck or crane access?

Yes, and in most of Dallas that is the only option. Everything is climbed, rigged, and lowered in controlled pieces, with the drop zone matted and beds boarded. It takes longer than a suburban job on the same tree and the quote reflects that up front.

My tree overhangs my neighbor's roof. What can I do?

In Texas you can generally trim back to your property line, but cutting in a way that damages or kills the tree can create liability, and the safest route with a shared mature tree is to talk to the neighbor first. We handle these regularly and will tell you plainly what can be taken without harming the tree.

Do you top trees?

No. Topping cuts a tree back to stubs rather than to a branch union, and the tree answers with dozens of weak shoots that break in five to ten years. It leaves wounds too big to seal, so decay works down into the trunk. If a Dallas tree is genuinely too big for its spot we will reduce it properly or tell you it needs replacing, but we will not top it.

Do I need a permit to trim a tree in Dallas?

Routine trimming on private property generally does not require a permit, though removals of protected trees can. Because Dallas ordinances differ by property and tree, we check the current requirement for your address during the free estimate rather than assuming.

Do you clean everything up?

Yes, and it is included. Limbs and chips leave with us, beds and turf are raked, and drives and walks are blown clean. Nothing is stacked at the curb for you to deal with.
